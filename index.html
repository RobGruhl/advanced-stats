Why Switching Wins 2/3 of the Time

When you first pick, there's a 1/3 chance you're right and a 2/3 chance the prize is behind one of the other doors. The host opening an empty door doesn't change those odds — it just concentrates the 2/3 onto one remaining door.

Strategy: Stay

1/3
You win only if your original pick was correct.
That was a 1-in-3 shot.

Strategy: Switch

2/3
You win whenever your original pick was wrong.
That happens 2 out of 3 times.
The key insight: The host always reveals an empty door, and always avoids your door. This isn't random — it's giving you information. The host is effectively saying: "If the prize isn't behind your door, it's behind this one."

Now Imagine 100 Doors

You pick door #1. The host opens 98 other doors, all empty. Only your door and one other remain closed. Still want to stay?

Pick any door to start.
With 100 doors: Your initial pick had a 1% chance. The other 99 doors collectively had a 99% chance. The host opened 98 of those, funneling all 99% onto that single remaining door. Switching wins 99% of the time. The 3-door version works the same way — 2/3 just doesn't feel as dramatic as 99/100.

The General Pattern

With N doors, your initial pick has a 1/N chance. After the host opens N−2 doors, switching gives you (N−1)/N. Drag the slider to see how the advantage scales.

Baccarat House Edge

The best bet in the casino is still a losing bet.

How Baccarat Works

Two hands are dealt — Player and Banker. Each gets two cards, sometimes a third. Closest to 9 wins. You bet on which side wins, or on a tie. Card values: A = 1, 2–9 = face value, 10/J/Q/K = 0. Only the last digit of the sum counts (e.g., 7 + 8 = 15 → 5).

If either hand totals 8 or 9 on the first two cards, it's a natural — no more cards are dealt. Otherwise, fixed drawing rules determine whether a third card is drawn. You don't choose — the rules are automatic.

Third Card Rules

Player draws on 0–5, stands on 6–7. Simple.

Banker’s rule is more complex — it depends on the Player’s third card:

When Player stands (6–7), Banker follows the same simple rule: draws on 0–5, stands on 6–7.

Why is Banker better despite the commission? Banker acts second and its drawing rules use information from the Player's third card. This gives Banker a structural edge (45.86% vs 44.62%). The 5% commission exists precisely to tax that advantage — but even after the tax, Banker has the lowest house edge in the game.
